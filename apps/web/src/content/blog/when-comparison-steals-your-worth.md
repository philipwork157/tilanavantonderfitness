<p class="article-intro"><span class="drop-cap">M</span><span>y close friends and family will probably laugh when they see the topic of this week’s blog.</span></p>

If you’ve known me for a while, you’ll know that social media and I have had a rather complicated relationship.

I’ve deleted Instagram. Reinstalled Instagram. Deleted it again. Decided I was completely done with social media. Returned. Left again.

For a long time, I blamed social media itself. I didn’t like what it had become, and I especially didn’t like what it sometimes brought out in me.

**Comparison.**

But maybe you’ve felt it too.

You open your phone for what feels like five minutes, and without even realising it, you begin measuring your life against someone else’s.

Their body.

Their career.

Their home.

Their finances.

Their marriage.

Their motherhood.

Their fitness.

Their appearance.

Their success.

And suddenly, things you were completely content with five minutes earlier don’t feel good enough anymore.

Nothing in your actual life has changed.

Your home is still the same home you loved this morning. Your body is still the same body that carried you through your day. Your family hasn’t changed. Your achievements haven’t disappeared.

But somehow, after ten minutes of looking at everyone else’s life, the way you feel about your own has changed.

That’s the dangerous thing about comparison.

Social media gives you access to thousands of people’s lives, but you’re usually seeing only tiny pieces of them. Beautiful homes. Holidays. Successful businesses. Strong bodies. Perfect family photographs. Personal bests. Promotions. Achievements.

And none of those things are wrong.

Someone else’s beautiful life isn’t the problem.

The problem begins when you look at someone else’s life and use it as evidence that yours isn’t enough.

About two months ago, something started changing in the way I looked at all of this.

I began to understand something incredibly simple:

**We are all living in different seasons.**

And perhaps you need that reminder too.

What matters deeply to you right now may not matter to somebody else. What another person has the capacity to pursue right now might not be something you have the capacity to pursue.

And that’s okay.

Maybe you’re pregnant and your body can’t do what it normally does.

Maybe you’re raising young children and you’re exhausted.

Maybe you’re building a career.

Maybe you’re trying to fall pregnant.

Maybe you’re recovering from something nobody knows about.

Maybe you’re rebuilding financially.

Maybe you’re caring for someone you love.

Maybe you’re getting older and looking in the mirror wondering where the younger version of yourself went.

Maybe you’re watching someone live the life you desperately want and quietly asking yourself:

*“Why am I not there yet?”*

But perhaps the better question is:

**Who gets to determine your worth?**

Because if you allow social media to answer that question, the finish line will constantly move.

There will always be someone fitter.

Someone younger.

Someone wealthier.

Someone further ahead in business.

Someone with a bigger home.

Someone running faster.

Someone seemingly managing motherhood better.

Someone whose life photographs beautifully.

And if those things determine your worth, you can spend your entire life trying to become enough.

But you were never meant to measure your worth that way.

This is where my faith has challenged me deeply.

The Bible doesn’t tell you that your worth comes from your body, your achievements, your bank account, your house, your career or how impressive your life looks from the outside.

Psalm 139 reminds you that you are fearfully and wonderfully made.

1 Samuel 16:7 reminds you that while people look at outward appearances, God looks at the heart.

Ephesians 2:10 reminds you that you are God’s workmanship, created with purpose.

Think about how different that is from what the world sometimes tells you.

The world says:

More.

More successful.

More beautiful.

More productive.

More followers.

More money.

More achievements.

More.

And God reminds you that your worth was never supposed to be earned in the first place.

Comparison can become incredibly powerful when you give it enough space in your mind.

And sometimes it becomes more than a social media battle. It becomes a spiritual one.

You have to become aware of the thoughts you’re allowing to take root.

If something repeatedly makes you question your worth, your purpose or the life God has given you, perhaps it’s worth asking why you’re giving it so much power.

You weren’t created to spend your life chasing somebody else’s version of enough.

You were created to actually live yours.

To be present with your children.

To love the people around you.

To treat people well.

To look after the body you’ve been given.

To pursue the things placed on your heart.

To grow.

To serve.

To laugh.

To rest.

To build.

To love.

And yes, you’re still allowed to want more for yourself.

You can want to become stronger.

You can want to improve your health.

You can want to grow your career.

You can want to change your body.

You can want to build a business.

You can dream bigger.

Wanting to grow doesn’t mean you’re ungrateful for where you are.

But there is a difference between wanting to grow and believing you need to change before you’re worthy.

Please don’t confuse the two.

There is another side to social media too.

You can be intentional about what you allow into your mind.

You can follow people who educate you, inspire you, encourage you, make you laugh or remind you of what’s important.

And sometimes you may need to unfollow something.

Not because that person has done anything wrong.

Maybe they’re wonderful.

Maybe their content is beautiful.

Maybe they’ve worked incredibly hard for what they have.

But perhaps watching them in this particular season isn’t healthy for you.

And that’s okay.

Protecting your mind doesn’t require judging someone else’s life.

Maybe you simply need to create a little more space to appreciate your own.

That’s also one of the reasons I eventually returned to social media.

Instead of constantly fighting against it, I realised I could decide what I wanted my little corner of it to represent.

Real life.

Not negativity.

Not complaining.

Not pretending that struggles are the only things worth talking about.

Just honesty.

Because I don’t want you to look at a fitness page and believe that health belongs only to people with perfect bodies, perfect routines, perfect meals and perfectly organised lives.

It doesn’t.

Health can exist in the middle of pregnancy.

Motherhood.

Work.

Stress.

Ageing.

Changing bodies.

Missed workouts.

Busy weeks.

Starting businesses.

Sleepless nights.

Ordinary life.

Sometimes you’ll train incredibly well.

Sometimes you won’t.

Sometimes your meals will go exactly according to plan.

Sometimes they won’t.

Sometimes you’ll look in the mirror and feel strong and confident.

Sometimes you’ll need to remind yourself of everything you’re reading right now.

That doesn’t make you less worthy.

It makes you human.

I don’t want you to look at someone else’s page and think:

*“I could never be like her.”*

I want you to look at your own life and realise:

*“I don’t need to be her. I need to become the best version of me.”*

Because health shouldn’t require a perfect life.

And worth certainly shouldn’t require one.

Think about yourself many years from now.

When you look back on your life, what will actually matter?

Will you wish you had spent more time worrying about somebody else’s body?

Will you wish your house had looked more impressive online?

Will you wish you had more followers?

Or will you wish you had been more present?

More grateful?

More willing to enjoy the season you were actually living?

Don’t miss your pregnancy because you’re comparing your changing body to somebody else’s.

Don’t miss your children’s childhood because you’re worried about whether you’re achieving enough.

Don’t miss your beautiful ordinary home because someone online has a bigger one.

Don’t overlook the people sitting around your table because you’re watching somebody else’s family through a screen.

And don’t become so consumed with what God is doing in someone else’s life that you fail to notice what He is doing in yours.

Maybe that’s the reminder you need this week:

Someone else’s season doesn’t make yours less valuable.

Someone else’s beauty doesn’t take away yours.

Someone else’s success doesn’t mean you’ve failed.

Someone else’s strength doesn’t make you weak.

Someone else’s progress doesn’t mean you’re behind.

And someone else’s life does not determine the value of your own.

You may never completely stop comparing yourself.

I’m certainly still learning too.

But perhaps the goal isn’t to never compare again.

Perhaps it’s learning to recognise comparison before you allow it to steal something from you.

Your joy.

Your confidence.

Your gratitude.

Your presence.

Your worth.

So the next time you find yourself scrolling and suddenly feeling like your life isn’t enough, put your phone down for a moment.

Look around.

Look at the people you love.

Look at how far you’ve come.

Look at what your body has carried you through.

Look at the things you’ve survived.

Look at the things you’re building.

Look at the ordinary moments that would never make it onto Instagram but mean absolutely everything to you.

And remind yourself:

**You are not behind. You are living your own season.**

Your worth cannot be measured by a number on a scale, a number in a bank account, a number of followers, the size of your home or somebody else’s opinion of your life.

There is so much more to you than what could ever fit into a photograph on a screen.

So don’t become so distracted by someone else’s life that you forget to live yours.
