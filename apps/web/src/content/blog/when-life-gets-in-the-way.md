<p class="article-intro"><span class="drop-cap">T</span><span>his week humbled me.</span></p>

I had plans. I had workouts scheduled. I had meals prepared in my mind. I had every intention of ticking every box. And then life happened.

As a mother, as a wife, and as someone trying to build a business while growing another little life inside me, I've realised something: life rarely asks for permission before it changes your plans.

Some days my toddler needs me more than my training does. Some days work takes longer than expected. Some days I'm simply exhausted. And if I'm honest, those are usually the days where the little voice in my head becomes the loudest.

*"You've already missed today."*

*"You may as well start again on Monday."*

*"You're never going to reach your goals like this."*

Maybe you've heard that voice too.

For years I believed that success belonged to people who never missed a workout, never ate the slice of cake, never had an off week. But the older I get, and the more seasons of life I walk through, the more I realise that couldn't be further from the truth.

Success doesn't belong to the person who never falls behind. It belongs to the person who refuses to stay there.

This week I caught myself spiralling. Not because I had failed, but because I believed one imperfect day somehow erased all the good days before it. Isn't that strange? We can be consistent for months, yet one difficult day convinces us that we've lost everything.

The reality is this: one workout never made you fit. One healthy meal never transformed your body. One difficult day will never undo all the work you've already put in.

I recently listened to Nick Bare talking about goals, and something he said really stayed with me. The biggest goals are built over years of investment. Not weeks. Not one challenge. Not one perfect month.

**Years.**

That completely changed my perspective.

Because if my goal is years away, why would I allow one difficult Tuesday to convince me to quit?

I've started replacing one sentence in my mind. Instead of saying, *"I'll start over,"* I tell myself, *"I'll continue."*

Those two words have become incredibly powerful.

Because I don't need another Monday.

I don't need another "fresh start."

I don't need to punish myself for being human.

I simply need to make the next good choice.

Maybe that's a workout.

Maybe it's drinking enough water.

Maybe it's choosing a nourishing meal.

Maybe it's getting to bed a little earlier.

Maybe it's simply deciding not to give up.

Whatever it is, it's enough.

One of the quotes I've been holding onto this week is incredibly simple:

**"If not today, it's okay."**

Not because tomorrow is an excuse, but because life happens.

It reminds me that one missed workout doesn't make me lazy. One emotional meal doesn't make me unhealthy. One difficult week doesn't make me a failure.

It simply makes me human.

Our lives are constantly changing. There will always be busy seasons. There will be pregnancies, newborn nights, sick children, demanding careers, unexpected bills, celebrations, heartbreak, and moments where simply making it through the day is the biggest achievement.

If we keep waiting for life to become perfect before we commit to our health, we'll spend our whole lives waiting.

The strongest people I know aren't the ones who have perfect routines.

They're the ones who keep coming back.

Again.

And again.

And again.

That is what consistency really looks like.

Not perfection.

Persistence.

One thing I'm learning is that our health journey isn't measured by our worst day. It's measured by what we choose to do after it.

So if you're reading this after a week that didn't go to plan...

If you missed your workouts...

If your nutrition wasn't what you hoped for...

If you've been speaking to yourself in ways you would never speak to someone you love...

Pause for a moment.

Take a deep breath.

Then remind yourself:

You haven't lost your progress.

You haven't ruined your journey.

You don't need to earn another beginning.

You already have one with your very next decision.

Keep showing up.

Keep choosing yourself.

Keep taking the next step.

Because your future isn't built by perfect days. It's built by ordinary days where you decided not to give up.

I truly believe that strength isn't measured by how many perfect days we string together. It's measured by our willingness to get back up after the imperfect ones.

Your goals were never meant to be achieved in a straight line. They are built over months. Over years. Sometimes even over decades. Every workout, every balanced meal, every walk, every choice to begin again—or rather, to continue—becomes another brick laid in the foundation of the person you're becoming.

So today, if all you can do is take one small step forward, let that be enough.

Don't start over.

Continue.

Because this journey isn't about perfection.

It's about becoming stronger through every season of life.
