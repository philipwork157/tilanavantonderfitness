Healthy eating does not have to begin with a perfect meal plan. A useful starting point is simply asking what you can add to make a meal more satisfying.

## Think in building blocks

For an everyday meal, look for a source of protein, a carbohydrate that gives you energy, vegetables or fruit, and a little fat or flavour that makes the meal enjoyable.

You do not need to hit a perfect formula every time. Variety across the day and week matters more than making every plate identical.

## Make the easier choice easier

Keep a few low-effort basics available: eggs, yoghurt, tinned beans, frozen vegetables, fruit, wholegrain bread, and a protein your family enjoys. Convenience is not a failure; sometimes it is what makes a nourishing choice possible.

## Leave room for real life

Food is nourishment, but it is also culture, pleasure, connection, and convenience. A sustainable pattern needs space for all of those things.

This is general healthy-eating education, not a personalised meal plan or medical nutrition advice. Speak to a registered dietitian for individual needs or health conditions.
